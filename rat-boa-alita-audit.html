Executive Summary

The Alita Dress PDP delivers a strong editorial foundation — the custom Shopify Plus theme, video-enabled gallery, and on-brand copywriting are genuine assets. But four conversion leaks are costing revenue at the point of highest intent. There is no sticky add-to-cart on either device; BNPL sits below the fold with no interactive functionality; social proof is absent; and Nosto-sourced visitors — arriving already warm from a campaign placement — land on a generic PDP that doesn't mirror the personalisation logic that drove the click. Fixing all four requires no redesign. It is a sequencing, development, and content problem — entirely solvable within a focused sprint.

1
Section One

Above the Fold

Video gallery asset is present but not prioritised

Impact: Med Effort: Low

A video asset in the gallery is a significant advantage for bias-cut silk silhouettes — movement is what sells the drape. However, if the video is buried as a secondary gallery item rather than auto-playing as the first media asset, a meaningful proportion of mobile users will never see it. The still image doing the primary selling job is the harder job.

Recommendation: Move the video to position one in the media sequence on mobile. A 4–6 second silent loop autoplay functions as an ambient showcase without feeling intrusive. Test video-first vs image-first as an A/B.

Price and BNPL are not sharing the same above-fold frame

Impact: High Effort: Low

At a £200–300+ price point, the moment a customer sees the price is the moment their brain begins resolving affordability. BNPL (Klarna/Clearpay) placed immediately adjacent to the price — ideally inline, showing the instalment amount — short-circuits that calculation before it becomes a barrier. Currently, BNPL sits below fold with no functional interactivity, which means it is providing no conversion lift at the moment it is most needed.

Recommendation: Inject a single-line BNPL callout directly beneath the price: "or 4 payments of £X with Klarna — learn more." The "learn more" should open a Klarna lightbox. This is a Shopify Klarna widget configuration change — no custom dev required.

Nosto traffic arrives without personalisation continuity

Impact: High Effort: Med

The URL parameters (nosto_source=cmp&ref-placement=27506717) confirm this PDP visit originates from a Nosto campaign placement. These visitors have already demonstrated intent — Nosto surfaces products based on behavioural signals. But the PDP itself treats every visitor identically. There is no "completing the look," no "customers who bought this also viewed," no contextual editorial framing triggered by the Nosto source. The warm signal is collected and then discarded.

Recommendation: Configure a Nosto onsite content segment for CMP-sourced visits. Surface a contextual cross-sell rail — editorially framed, not algorithmically generic — and if Nosto's recommendation logic knows which collection or campaign drove the click, mirror that in the PDP's secondary content.

Section Two

Conversion Rate Optimisation

No sticky add-to-cart — the single highest-impact fix on this page

Impact: High Effort: Low

Fashion PDPs generate long scroll sessions — customers read copy, study gallery assets, check size guidance, scroll reviews, then decide. Without a sticky ATC, the moment of decision (which often arrives after scroll) requires the user to reverse back up the page. Every scroll reversal is friction. On mobile, where the ATC is typically buried below size selector, trust bar, and description, this is a material conversion suppressor.

Recommendation: Implement a sticky ATC bar that appears after the native button scrolls out of the viewport. On mobile: full-width bar pinned to the bottom, showing product name + selected size + ATC button. On desktop: slim bar fixed to the top on scroll, showing price + selected size + ATC. This is a single Shopify section addition — approximately 1–2 days' dev.

Size guide and fit quiz are discoverable but not decision-proximate

Impact: Med Effort: Low

Placing the size guide and fit quiz below the size selector asks customers to first encounter the choice, feel uncertain, then scroll to find the tool that resolves the uncertainty. The preferable sequencing is: tool visible and immediately adjacent to the size options, so the decision and the aid to decision occupy the same screen zone. Trustpilot reviews cite sizing inconsistency repeatedly — the fit quiz is the brand's best answer to this, but only if customers find it before they abandon.

Recommendation: Move the "Size Guide / Fit Quiz" link to sit inline with the size selector row, right-aligned, in the same visual zone as the size option buttons. A small text link is sufficient — no design overhaul needed. Consider adding a model measurement note directly beneath: "Model is 5'9" / 175cm, wearing a UK 10."

No social proof layer — a missed trust opportunity, not a luxury constraint

Impact: High Effort: Med

The assumption that luxury brands should not display reviews is a misreading of the evidence. Ganni, Reiss, Reformation, and Sézane all surface social proof on PDPs — the distinction is form, not presence. What damages luxury credibility is the Amazon aesthetic: yellow stars, aggregate scores above the fold, pagination widgets. What builds it is curated narrative proof: a select testimonial, on-figure UGC (which Nosto can serve), or a press quote. The Alita Dress PDP currently asks a customer to spend £200+ with zero independent corroboration.

Recommendation: Introduce two proof formats — (1) a single curated editorial testimonial block below the description, styled to brand (no star ratings, no review count), and (2) a shoppable UGC carousel sourced via Nosto or a dedicated app (Emplifi, Bazaarvoice Curations), showing the dress as worn. This is the proof format that converts luxury customers — it's aspirational, not transactional.

Section Three

Brand Presentation

Editorial copy strength is real — extend it into craft storytelling

Impact: Med Effort: Low

Rat & Boa's product copy is a genuine differentiator — brand-voiced, sensory, and character-led. Across other PDPs, the bias-cut technique is explained with context ("allows fabric to mould to the body — a draped effect integral to our brand DNA"), which is the kind of craft detail that justifies premium pricing and drives brand conviction. If this explanatory layer is absent on the Alita PDP, it is a missed opportunity to deepen the emotional case for purchase alongside the aesthetic one.

Recommendation: Ensure the Alita's copy includes at least one craft or material detail that grounds the editorial voice in tangible quality — fabric composition, construction technique, or design intent. Copy additions at this level require no dev work.

Loyalty Lion and returns trust gap — purchase confidence is lowest for first-timers

Impact: Med Effort: Low

Rat & Boa's Trustpilot record contains a visible returns and CS thread. First-time visitors — who represent the majority of Nosto CMP-sourced traffic — have no prior experience to counter this. The trust bar on the PDP is the primary instrument for addressing this anxiety pre-purchase. If it does not include a clear, specific returns statement (e.g., "Free 30-day returns"), it is leaving a resolution gap for the brand's most commonly cited concern.

Recommendation: Review the trust bar content. It should include: (1) returns policy with the return window explicitly stated, (2) a Loyalty Lion points reward signal for returning customers ("Earn X points on this purchase"), and (3) a delivery estimate. Specificity over genericism — "Free returns within 30 days" converts better than "Easy returns."

Section Four

UX / UI

Mobile gallery swipe and video interaction

Impact: Med Effort: Low

For a mobile-first brand (confirmed by the DBCO case study), the gallery interaction model is critical. On bias-cut dresses, customers want to see the garment from multiple angles and in motion. If the video is a swipe-to-reach asset on mobile rather than a feature-first asset, the majority of users will form their purchase decision on still photography alone. Gallery depth and sequencing should be treated as conversion architecture, not art direction.

Recommendation: Audit the mobile gallery sequence: video should be asset 1 or 2, not buried behind 4–5 stills. Consider a "video" indicator dot or play-icon thumbnail in the gallery row so users know video is available before they swipe.

Sold-out size state and back-in-stock capture

Impact: Med Effort: Low

For limited-run styles, sold-out sizes represent suppressed demand that can be captured with a simple back-in-stock notification. If sold-out sizes are simply greyed out with no recovery mechanism, the customer exits the page with no pathway to conversion. Given Rat & Boa's drop-driven model, back-in-stock flows via Klaviyo are a natural fit and a well-documented revenue recovery mechanism.

Recommendation: Implement a back-in-stock notification trigger on sold-out size options — a single click to register interest, feeding into a Klaviyo back-in-stock flow. The UI ask is minimal: a "Notify me" link adjacent to each sold-out size.

Section Five

Content & Merchandising

Cross-sell rail — editorially framed, not algorithmically generic

Impact: Med Effort: Med

Nosto is installed and capable of surfacing personalised cross-sells — but the editorial quality of the cross-sell presentation matters as much as the algorithmic logic behind it. A generic "You May Also Like" carousel, if styled as an afterthought, creates a discount-retailer register that undercuts the brand. The DBCO case study references shoppable editorials and immersive lookbooks — this is the right model for PDP cross-sells too.

Recommendation: Rename the cross-sell module to something brand-native ("The Edit" or "Also From This Collection") and ensure Nosto is configured to prioritise collection and style affinity over purchase history alone for new visitors. Editorial framing elevates the AOV conversation.

Care and sustainability content absent from most fashion PDPs — an easy differentiator

Impact: Med Effort: Low

At the £200+ price point, customers are implicitly asking: is this worth it? Care instructions, fabric provenance, and any sustainability positioning answer this question in the product's favour. They also reduce returns from misuse — which, given Rat & Boa's Trustpilot quality feedback, would be an additional operational benefit.

Recommendation: Add a collapsible "Care & Materials" accordion below the description. Content: fabric composition, care method (hand wash / dry clean), and any sustainability notes (e.g., deadstock fabric use if applicable). This is a content addition — no dev required beyond the accordion component, which is likely already in the theme.

Section Six

SEO & Technical

Nosto tracking parameters require canonical URL configuration

Impact: Med Effort: Low

The URL ?nosto_source=cmp&ref-placement=27506717 is a Nosto tracking parameter. If Google is crawling and indexing this parameterised variant as a distinct URL, it creates a duplicate content scenario — two URLs competing for the same ranking signals and potentially splitting link equity. Shopify's canonical tag handling should strip query parameters for the canonical declaration.

Recommendation: Verify that the <link rel="canonical"> tag on this PDP resolves to /products/alita-dress without query parameters. In Google Search Console, confirm the parameterised version is not being indexed. Nosto parameters should additionally be added to Google's URL parameter handling tool in Search Console.

Product structured data — review schema and availability signals

Impact: Med Effort: Low

Fashion PDPs benefit materially from rich results in Google — price, availability, and (if reviews are added) aggregate rating can appear in SERP snippets, increasing click-through rate from organic search. Shopify's default schema is basic; a custom Shopify Plus implementation should ensure Product schema includes offers with availability, priceCurrency, and itemCondition at minimum.

Recommendation: Run the page through Google's Rich Results Test. If Product schema is present but incomplete, add the missing fields. If UGC social proof is added in line with Section 2 recommendations, AggregateRating schema should be implemented alongside it to unlock star-display in SERP results.

Test & Learn

Strategic Experiments

🎞

Experiment 1 — Video-first vs Image-first gallery (A/B)

Hypothesis: mobile sessions that encounter the video asset as the first gallery item will spend more time on page and convert at a higher rate than those encountering a still first.

Metric: Add-to-cart rate (primary), session duration and gallery interaction depth (secondary). Run for 2–3 weeks at sufficient traffic volume to reach statistical significance.
💬

Experiment 2 — Editorial testimonial vs no testimonial (A/B)

Test a single curated testimonial block below the product description against the current no-proof state. The testimonial should be voice-matched to the brand — specific, sensory, and first-person — not a generic "great dress, fast delivery" pull-quote.

Metric: Conversion rate (primary). Scroll depth to testimonial zone to confirm visibility before attributing effect.
🧭

Experiment 3 — Nosto CMP segment: personalised PDP vs standard PDP

For visitors arriving via nosto_source=cmp, test a PDP variant that surfaces a contextual cross-sell rail and a campaign-relevant editorial callout against the standard PDP. Nosto's split testing or Shopify's native A/B tooling can instrument this without a full rebuild.

Metric: Conversion rate and AOV for CMP-sourced sessions vs direct/organic sessions. Expected lift: 8–15% based on comparable personalisation continuity experiments in fashion DTC.
