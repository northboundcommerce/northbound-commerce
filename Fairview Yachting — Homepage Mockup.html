Executive Summary

The gap between the brand's ambition and its digital presence is significant — and closeable.

Fairview Yachting operates in the premium segment of the global yacht charter and sales market with a genuinely differentiated offering: multi-destination coverage across Thailand, the UK, and Hong Kong, an ownership programme, and a heritage of deep sailing expertise. But its website tells none of that story. What visitors find instead is a functional, template-driven WordPress build that communicates mid-market tourism rather than curated luxury experience. The colour palette, typography, content strategy, and interaction design all work against the brand.

This document identifies the specific points of failure, defines a new visual and editorial language, and delivers a homepage concept that closes the gap. The reference aesthetic is premium hospitality — Rosewood Hotels, Six Senses, Aman — rather than the yacht brokerage category, because Fairview's real competition for the high-net-worth client is other luxury experiences, not other charter companies.

Brand Perception
Critical

Reads as a tourist charter company, not a luxury brand

The dominant teal/turquoise navbar and footer communicate tropical tourism — the colour of a snorkelling brochure. The hero imagery shows people jumping off boat decks in swimwear. The first large headline visible on scroll is "Official Dealer of Bali Catamarans and GranOcean". Together these signals place the brand firmly in the mid-market holiday-charter space, making it near-impossible for a discerning HNW visitor to self-identify with the brand.

Specific issue: A pop-up appears mid-scroll offering "Earn 10% Guaranteed Income on Bali Catamaran Ownership" — investment framing, not experience framing. This is the single biggest luxury-perception killer on the site.
Colour Palette
Critical

Saturated teal is the wrong signal for premium

The primary brand colour — a bright cyan-teal (#1abc9c / #00bcd4 territory) — is warm, accessible, and friendly. These are not luxury brand values. Premium hospitality and maritime brands use restraint: deep navy, warm ivory, antique brass, and chalk. The current palette reads like a package holiday booking site.

Typography
Critical

Generic web fonts with no editorial character

The site uses what appears to be a default WordPress theme font stack — functional but completely devoid of character. There is no display typeface for editorial headings, no serif for warmth, and no visual hierarchy that guides the eye. Compare to Burgess Yachts or the Rosewood Hotels site, both of which use sophisticated serif/sans pairings to immediately signal the brand tier.

Quick fix potential: Introducing a single display typeface (Cormorant Garamond or similar) across headlines would move brand perception significantly, even before a full redesign.
Layout & Hierarchy
High Priority

Repetitive horizontal sections with no compositional tension

Every section of the homepage uses the same alternating image-left/text-right two-column layout with a heavy teal divider line under headings. This pattern is the signature aesthetic of a WordPress Elementor template. Luxury brands use editorial asymmetry: full-bleed moments, unexpected proportions, generous whitespace, and deliberate pacing. The eye has nowhere to rest and nothing to anticipate.

The page also lacks hierarchy: the "About" copy, a blog post excerpt, a mailing list signup, and a partner logos strip all occupy the same visual weight. There is no single focal moment the site is building towards.

Imagery & Art Direction
High Priority

Photography is capable but unstyled — no cohesive art direction

The photography itself is not without quality — there are genuinely beautiful shots of Phang Nga Bay. But the selection and sequencing is inconsistent: casual action shots mix with serene lifestyle imagery, different colour grades sit side by side, and images are cropped with no editorial intent. There is no dominant "look" that a visitor would leave with.

Luxury brands maintain an obsessive consistency of mood: golden hour light, desaturated blues, specific focal lengths, consistent subject positioning. The editorial layer — how images are selected, sequenced, and cropped — matters as much as the photography itself.

Navigation & User Flow
Medium Priority

Functional navigation, but no journey architecture

The four-item navigation (Yacht Charter / Yacht Sales / Ownership Program / About) is clear and logical. But it's purely transactional — it routes by service type, not by visitor intent or emotional state. A prospective charter client thinking "where could I go?" is not primed to click "Yacht Charter" as their first action. Luxury hospitality sites lead with destinations, experiences, and stories, then introduce services as the means to access them.

Missing: There is no clear primary CTA in the navigation or hero — no "Enquire", "Plan Your Journey", or equivalent. The visitor has no obvious next step.
Mobile Experience
Medium Priority

Responsive but not designed for mobile-first luxury

The site adapts to mobile adequately through standard WordPress responsive behaviour, but it was clearly designed desktop-first. HNW clients browsing during travel or leisure time are primarily on mobile. A luxury mobile experience requires thumb-friendly CTAs, full-bleed portrait photography, minimal chrome, and seamless enquiry forms. The current mobile experience is compressed desktop — not designed for it.

Trust & Luxury Signalling
Critical

Active trust detractors outweigh trust builders

Luxury brands build trust through restraint, exclusivity signals, and authority cues. The current site does the opposite:

❌ Trust detractor: Floating WhatsApp green button (signals informal/budget)
❌ Trust detractor: "Earn 10% Guaranteed Income" pop-up (investment pitch, not experience)
❌ Trust detractor: Partner logos section with generic sailing schools
❌ Trust detractor: "Sign Up For Our Mailing List" with standard form field styling
✓ Opportunity: Three-office global presence (Phuket, Hong Kong, Hampshire) is a strong credibility signal — currently buried in the footer
Conversion Design
High Priority

No clear enquiry journey — the site doesn't sell

A luxury purchase is a high-consideration, relationship-led process. The site should be architected to guide visitors from aspiration to enquiry over multiple sections, not present a "Book Now" button and hope. Currently there is no primary CTA visible in the hero, no enquiry journey, and no mechanism to capture a warm lead short of a generic mailing list signup. The conversion funnel simply doesn't exist in a meaningful form.

Performance & Polish
Medium Priority

Standard WordPress performance — no luxury polish signals

The site loads at a functional speed for a WordPress build, but there are no luxury performance indicators: no smooth page transitions, no subtle scroll-based reveals, no micro-interactions on hover, no loading elegance. Premium brands treat every millisecond of the load sequence as part of the experience. The absence of any motion or animation also makes the site feel static and dated by luxury digital standards.

Section padding: min 96px top/bottom. Use 128px on hero, intro, and full-bleed editorial moments. Luxury is generous with space.

Motion & Animation Language

Scroll reveals
Slow fade + 20px upward translate. Duration: 0.8–1.2s. Easing: cubic-bezier(0.16, 1, 0.3, 1). Stagger children by 80ms. Never dramatic — always serene.
Image hover
Slow scale 1.0 → 1.04 on the image inside a clipped container. Duration: 0.6s. Overlay brightens very slightly. Caption slides up 8px. Feels considered, never eager.
CTA interactions
Ghost buttons: border opacity 0.4 → 1.0 on hover, background fill slides in from left (clip-path). Duration: 0.35s. No bounces, no transforms — only opacity and fill.
Hero video
Cinematic 16:9 or 2.35:1 cropped video, muted, looped, slow shutter feel. No people mid-action. Dawn or golden-hour. Kenburn zoom: 1.0 → 1.08 over 15s. Text fades in over 1.5s at 0.8s delay.
Page transitions
Full-page fade, ivory wash. Duration: 0.4s in, 0.4s out. Never a slide or a swipe — those feel mobile-app, not editorial web. The wash communicates composure.
Nav behaviour
Transparent over hero, transitions to ivory/midnight after ~80px scroll. Very slow: 0.5s transition. No shadow on nav — use a thin 1px bottom border at 5% opacity instead.

Art Direction — Photography Principles

Commission / select for
  • ✓ Golden hour / blue hour light — no midday sun
  • ✓ Empty or near-empty anchorages — exclusivity
  • ✓ Intimate scale — hands on a wheel, a glass of wine, morning light on teak
  • ✓ Silhouettes, reflections, atmospheric — mood over documentation
  • ✓ Desaturated, film-adjacent colour grade — never vivid Instagram
  • ✓ Cinematic aspect ratios: 16:9 or 2.35:1
Avoid entirely
  • ✕ People jumping, waving, or posing artificially
  • ✕ Oversaturated tropical turquoise water
  • ✕ Busy harbour shots with other vessels visible
  • ✕ Yacht manufacturer stock photography
  • ✕ Bright-sky overhead drone shots with no editorial intent
  • ✕ Marketing imagery from Bali / GranOcean brochures

Technical Requirements — from brief

Nausys API — Worldwide charter

Worldwide charter listings, live availability, and pricing fed via Nausys. AJAX-loaded results on the charter search page. Availability calendar on individual yacht PDPs. "Request to Book" enquiry flow rather than direct booking.

Rightboat API — Brokerage listings

Brokerage / pre-owned inventory served via Rightboat API. Grid and list view with filters: price, length, year, manufacturer, location. Each listing links to a detail page with specs, description, gallery, and contact form.

Multi-language — EN / RU / FR / ZH

English as default. WPML (recommended) or Polylang Pro. Language switcher in the nav — visible at all viewports. Russian and Chinese markets require dedicated content strategy, not auto-translation. Currency: € default, THB secondary display for Phuket fleet pages only.

Client portal — post-booking login

Restricted WordPress member area for existing clients. MVP scope: booking summary, crew list upload, extras selection, base info docs. Built via Ultimate Member or WP User Manager. Linked from nav as "My Fairview". Phase 2: real-time extras marketplace.

Live availability + "Request to Book"

Live availability calendar on all Phuket fleet pages (Nausys API or Beds24). Worldwide charter uses enquiry-based flow with availability shown where possible. Sticky enquiry bar on all yacht detail pages — visible at all times on desktop (right rail), fixed bottom bar on mobile.

CMS — WordPress with ACF Pro

Easily managed by Fairview's team. Key CPTs: Yachts, Destinations, Team, Testimonials. ACF Pro for spec fields and flexible content. Gravity Forms for all enquiry routing to CRM. WP Rocket + Cloudflare CDN for performance — especially important for Asia-Pacific load times.
