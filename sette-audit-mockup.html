Executive Summary

SETTE's homepage carries strong brand credentials — compelling photography, a clear founder narrative, and press coverage that signals genuine cultural traction. The gap is between the product's price point (£362–£3,499) and the signals the digital experience currently sends. Six structural changes close that gap: elevating the press section to match the brand's earned credibility, introducing consistent brand blue accents that reinforce identity, adding breathing room to product imagery so pieces are presented with appropriate ceremony, and removing the on-load discount popup that actively undermines positioning. The result is a homepage that feels proportionate to what SETTE is selling.

What Changed & Why

Was

Discount popup on load

"EXCLUSIVE DISCOUNT" overlay fires immediately, before the visitor has engaged with any product. Cheapens positioning at £2K+ average basket.

Now

Editorial email capture at page end

Clean brand-blue email capture section — same data collection, appropriate placement, copy that reflects the brand's editorial tone rather than urgency mechanics.

Was

Press logos as faint scrolling ticker

Vogue and WWD coverage appears in a barely visible fast-scrolling marquee bar. The credibility of the coverage is not translating into page-level trust.

Now

Prominent "As Seen In" section

A structured grid with each publication given its own tile, a subtle editorial quote, and a hover interaction — positioned prominently below the hero where it anchors the brand's credibility before the visitor reaches the product grid.

Was

Product images edge-to-edge

Product photography fills each card with no breathing room. At £1,370–£3,499, the lack of white space makes pieces feel like catalogue items rather than fine jewellery.

Now

Products float in white space

24px padding inside each product container. Images scale to contain. Pieces sit in space the way they would in a jeweller's presentation — the visual equivalent of placing a ring on a tray.

Was

Category navigation: Instagram circles

Circular thumbnails with thick indigo borders are a structural pattern borrowed from fast-fashion DTC. Incongruous next to pieces at this price point.

Now

Full-bleed editorial portrait tiles

SETTE's own photography presented as portrait tiles with gradient overlay, category name in thin italic serif, and a brand-blue reveal on hover. Editorial confidence rather than social-media browse.

Was

Brand blue used on buttons only

The brand indigo appears exclusively on CTA buttons. The rest of the page has no relationship with the brand colour, resulting in a disconnected visual identity.

Now

Brand blue as a consistent accent

Eyebrow labels, section links, promise strip dividers, the ethos quote mark, category tile hover states, founder section attribution, journal tags, and the email capture section all use the brand indigo — coherent without being heavy.

Was

No mobile layout

No responsive breakpoints. Navigation collapses, grids don't reflow, text overflows on small screens, touch targets are too small for reliable interaction.

Now

Mobile-first with three breakpoints

Full hamburger nav, 2-col product and category grids, snap-scroll testimonials and journal cards, stacked founder section, touch-appropriate targets (44px minimum) throughout — at 767px and 1023px.
