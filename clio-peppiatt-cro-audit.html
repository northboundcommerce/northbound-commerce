Context

A pre-order asking for patience — without earning it

At £650 with a 16-week lead time, this skirt demands real conviction from the buyer before they commit. The current page gives them almost nothing to build that conviction on. There are no reviews, no sizing confidence tools, a minimal trust set, and no narrative around the craftsmanship that justifies both the price and the wait.

Primary friction

A cold CTA with no surrounding reassurance

The sole call to action is "Pre-order & Receive Within 16 Weeks" — presented without explanation of what pre-ordering means for the buyer, without a cancellation policy snippet, and without any secondary fallback for customers who aren't ready to commit today. For a first-time visitor at this price point, that gap is enough to cause abandonment.

Sizing & fit

The most-asked question is hidden behind a tab

Sizing information lives inside a tab, requiring the customer to click out of the purchase flow to find it. No model measurements are visible at a glance, and there is no fit guidance on the page. For a fitted, minimal-stretch organza piece at this price, sizing anxiety is the leading cause of pre-purchase drop-off — and it's entirely unaddressed.

Social proof

Zero reviews or press — on a brand with real editorial heritage

There are no star ratings, no review count, no press quotes, and no editorial imagery on this page. Clio Peppiatt has a genuine following and an established profile in the luxury fashion press. None of that surfaces here. At this price and lead time, third-party validation is the single most powerful conversion lever available — and it is entirely absent.

Opportunity

Three focused changes would move the dial significantly

This is not a rebuild job. The brand equity is real, the product imagery is strong, and the design sensibility is clear. The highest-impact interventions are: reframing the pre-order CTA with a "made to order" narrative and adding a wishlist fallback for undecided buyers; surfacing the size guide and model measurements inline rather than behind a tab; and adding press quotes or review ratings above the fold. These three changes target the primary abandonment scenarios without altering the page structure.

04 Email capture popup fires mid-browse on the product page The "Coming Soon, London" newsletter modal triggers during active product consideration. Interrupting a buyer while they are evaluating a £650 purchase is one of the highest-friction patterns in ecommerce. The capture opportunity is valid — the timing is not. Moving this to exit-intent or post-checkout would preserve the value without the conversion cost. High Quick win
05 No wishlist or save functionality Many buyers considering a 16-week pre-order at this price will want to revisit the decision. There is no mechanism to save the product — no wishlist button, no "notify me" option. A secondary CTA that captures that intent and enables re-engagement would be a low-effort addition with a meaningful impact on return visits and eventual purchase rate. Medium Quick win
06 "Complete the set" lives only as a mention in the product description The description references the matching Rosaline Blouse (£850), but there is no visual cross-sell module in the purchase panel. The "You may also like" carousel appears far below the fold with no bridal framing. The matching set represents a £1,500 basket opportunity and a natural narrative for the brand — neither is being activated. Medium Strategic
07 Returns policy not visible without a tab click The 14-day returns window is a meaningful reassurance signal for anyone committing to a pre-order, but it requires the buyer to click into the Shipping & Returns tab to find it. It should be visible in the trust strip beneath the CTA — it is already policy and costs nothing to surface. Medium Quick win
08 Gallery thumbnails are small and lack editorial or lifestyle context The thumbnail rail renders at around 40px wide — barely functional. More significantly, the gallery appears to lead with flat product shots only. Lifestyle and editorial imagery are the strongest visual trust signals on a fashion PDP, showing the garment in motion and in context. The brand's editorial work is strong; it should be in the gallery. Medium Strategic
09 Colour swatch has no visible label The colour selector shows a swatch but no text label. The colourway is Ivory — a meaningful distinction in a bridal context where buyers may be matching to other pieces. Adding the colour name as a visible label is a single-line change that removes ambiguity at a high-stakes decision point. Low Quick win
10 No payment flexibility option near the price At £650, even a buyer who is close to convinced may hesitate on the upfront total. Displaying an instalment option — "From £162.50 / month with Klarna" — as a small line of text below the price would reduce the perceived barrier without undermining the brand's positioning. Shopify's Klarna placement tile makes this straightforward to implement. Low Quick win

Quick Wins

Six changes you could ship this sprint

Effort: Low  ·  Impact: High

Move the popup to exit-intent

Shift the email capture modal to trigger on exit-intent — when the cursor leaves the viewport — rather than during active product browsing. The capture opportunity stays; the interruption to purchase intent is removed.

Effort: Low  ·  Impact: High

Inline model measurements

Display "Model is 176cm and wears a size S" directly beneath the size picker as a single line of text. No tab required. Reduces sizing hesitation at the exact point where variant selection happens.

Effort: Low  ·  Impact: Medium

Label the colour swatch

Add "Ivory" as a visible text label next to the colour swatch. In a bridal context, the colourway is a considered detail — buyers may be matching to other pieces and need the label to be explicit.

Effort: Low  ·  Impact: Medium

Surface returns in the trust strip

Add "Free returns within 14 days of arrival" to the trust block beneath the CTA. This is already in policy — surfacing it here removes a primary objection without requiring any new functionality or copy decision.

Effort: Medium  ·  Impact: High

Reframe pre-order as made to order

Add a two-line explanatory banner: "Made to order — crafted to your size in 16 weeks. Cancel before production begins for a full refund." Converts a neutral wait into a personalised craft narrative that is consistent with the brand positioning.

Effort: Medium  ·  Impact: High

Add BNPL messaging near the price

Display instalment messaging in small text directly below the price point. Shopify's Klarna placement tile makes this a low-effort addition. For buyers who are nearly convinced, reducing the perceived upfront commitment can be the difference between hesitation and purchase.

Recommended Roadmap

A three-phase plan to lift PDP performance

Phase 1  ·  Weeks 1–2

Remove friction

  • Move email popup to exit-intent
  • Add colour name label beside swatch
  • Add model measurements inline under size picker
  • Add returns line to trust strip
  • Add BNPL instalment line below price
  • Add wishlist button as secondary CTA

Phase 2  ·  Weeks 3–5

Build trust

  • Integrate a review platform (Okendo or Judge.me) and surface star rating above the fold
  • Add pre-order narrative banner with cancellation policy
  • Build visual complete-the-set module in purchase panel
  • Add press or editorial quote strip from existing coverage
  • Add lifestyle and editorial imagery to the gallery

Phase 3  ·  Weeks 6–10

Convert & retain

  • A/B test CTA copy: "Pre-order Now" vs "Claim Your Size"
  • Test a size recommendation tool (Kiwi Sizing or similar)
  • Implement wishlist-to-email re-engagement flow
  • Set up abandoned PDP retargeting via email and SMS
  • Review analytics for drop-off points and iterate
