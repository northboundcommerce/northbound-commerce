The brand

Blue-chip inventory, sold through conversation

Maddox sells Banksy, Hockney, Kusama, Warhol and a strong roster of emerging artists to collectors who rarely check out online. The website's job is to start a qualified conversation with an advisor, then prove which channel started it.

The new Shopify build has good bones: clean product URLs, sensible canonicals on sorted and paginated collections, noindexed search results, BlogPosting and FAQ schema on articles, and legacy URLs mostly redirected. The gaps sit one layer down, in duplication, page states and the enquiry journey.

What we found

The gaps, before any mockup

Grouped by your brief's workstreams. Each “Current” point was checked on the live site; each recommendation can be shipped within Shopify's theme, apps or metaobjects.

1 · Technical SEO: indexation & canonicals
Current
Every artist has two indexable, self-canonical “for sale” pages, for example /collections/banksy (“Banksy Art for Sale & Artist Bio”) and /collections/available-works-by-banksy (“Banksy Prints & Originals for Sale”). Both sit in the sitemap for 75 artists, so each pair competes with itself
Shopify's vendor URL /collections/vendors?q=Banksy is indexable with its own canonical, which creates a third Banksy listing page
/pages/error-404 returns 200, is self-canonical and is listed in the sitemap, so it acts as a soft 404
Three utility pages (maddox-artists/articles/exhibitions-feed-master) return 200 with no title and no canonical, and all three are in the sitemap
Migration-era handles are live and indexable, e.g. /collections/artists-85-the-connor-brothers-categories-117 and /artists-28-david-yarrow-categories-16-gorillas-and-primates
Collection templates render two H1s (All Artworks, Pumpkins, Available Works by Banksy). Studio Sessions has no H1. Several pages fall back to “Maddox Gallery” as their meta description
The Exhibitions hub title and meta still promote Between Realms (17 Apr – 15 May 2026). Bibi Lei's dates read 14 Oct in the meta description and 16 Oct on the hub
Legacy /artworks redirects to the homepage rather than All Artworks, and article links still point at pre-migration /news/497-… URLs, adding a redirect hop to each
Our recommendations
Make one artist hub per artist the indexable page. The “available works” view becomes a filtered state or anchor on that hub, with its URL either 301'd or canonicalised to it. Merge the best copy from both pages
Noindex /collections/vendors and /collections in theme.liquid, and point the vendor links on artwork pages to the artist hub
Delete or noindex the 404 and feed-master pages, and keep any template-only page out of the sitemap with the seo.hidden metafield
Rename the legacy handles to clean slugs with 301s, then rerun a full crawl for redirect chains and orphaned collections
Keep one H1 per template and demote the duplicates to H2. Generate descriptions for every page type from metafields, with editable overrides
Drive the hub's SEO title from the current exhibition metaobject so it updates on its own, with dates set in one field and reused everywhere
Send /artworks to the agreed All Artworks page and update internal links to final URLs as part of the monthly health check
1 · Technical SEO: performance, scripts & tracking
Current
The LCP image is lazy-loaded with default priority on the homepage hero's first slide and on the artist-page hero (Banksy). The artwork page gets it right with fetchpriority="high"
reCAPTCHA Enterprise loads twice on every page: two site keys (6LdEws… and 6LeHG2…), two release builds of recaptcha__en.js and two hidden anchor iframes
The tag stack runs GTM plus three Google Ads accounts, GA4, a separate Google tag, Merchant Center, Meta, LinkedIn, Bing, Yahoo, Teads, Clarity, VWO, ClickCease, Klaviyo and Tidio. One artist page made about 460 requests
Ads, LinkedIn and Teads tags fire on first load, before the visitor has made any choice in the cookie banner. The banner has Statistics, Marketing and Preferences switched on by default and no “Reject all” on its first layer
libphonenumber-js (max build, from unpkg), moment and moment-timezone load site-wide to power one phone field and the booking widget
Article images are uploaded at full size (1,170–2,400px) with no srcset or lazy-loading, and several are raw screenshot PNGs
Our recommendations
Set the first hero slide and the artist hero to loading="eager" and fetchpriority="high" with correct sizes, and lazy-load every other slide
Keep one reCAPTCHA key, most likely the Klaviyo or form-app one, and load it only when a form opens
Run a GTM container audit: consolidate to one Ads account where possible, remove duplicate Google tags, and move the heaviest pixels to server-side GTM or Shopify Customer Events
Implement Consent Mode v2 with non-essential categories off by default and an equal-weight “Reject all” button. We'd ask your DPO to sign this off, since it's a compliance question as well as a data one
Load phone and date libraries on demand, swap to the lighter libphonenumber build, and self-host through Shopify's CDN
Add an article image snippet that serves Shopify CDN image_url widths with srcset, lazy-loading and caption and credit fields
2 · CRO: the enquiry journey
Current
A £72,000 Banksy carries Add to Bag, Enquire, Add to Wishlist and Share as equal actions. The price is hidden on the page but published in Product schema
Enquire opens a Klaviyo form with five fields, placeholder-only labels and no qualification such as budget, purpose, timeframe or preferred gallery
The form's consent text is US SMS boilerplate (“Msg/data rates may apply… Reply STOP”) and ties marketing consent to sending an enquiry
The live-chat prompt (“Are you enquiring about this artwork?”) opens over the artwork image on load. Enquiries are split across chat, Klaviyo forms, booking and two different London phone numbers
Artwork pages show no provenance, signature, certificate or condition details, which are the questions a buyer at this price asks first
Return figures don't match: 26.2% on the homepage, “28.2% Maddox average return” on the Banksy artwork page, and no methodology shown next to either
Our recommendations
Make one primary action, Speak to an advisor, with “Book a viewing” as a secondary text link. Keep Add to Bag only where a work is genuinely buy-now, and make the schema price match what the page shows
Build a two-step form: contact details first, then optional qualifiers such as budget band, collecting or investing, and preferred contact method. Visible labels throughout
Use UK-appropriate wording, with an optional, unticked marketing opt-in kept separate from the enquiry
Hold proactive chat for 30–45 seconds or until scroll intent. Route every channel into one generate_lead event with a channel parameter, and pull phone numbers from a single source of truth
Add a provenance and authenticity block driven by metafields, which hides itself when it's empty
Use one sourced figure with its methodology and a risk note, managed from one metaobject and reused in every Why Collect module
Shopify actionables: artist, exhibition & search templates
Current
Artist pages repeat the hero CTAs immediately underneath (“View available artworks” then “View available works”). On scroll, the sticky bar keeps the two big buttons and hides the section tabs. The Banksy page has 53 “Enquire” links and seven different CTA labels
Artist filters cover only Artwork Type and Price Band. There's nothing for series, signed or unsigned, size or framing
Past exhibitions keep an “Artworks on show” tab with no artworks (Mythomania, 2022). The archive lists INAE and the Damien Hirst show twice, runs out of date order and files Maddox Dubai under Past
Search for “girl with balloon” returns 16 results: three Banksys, then Jeff Koons Balloon works, “Crying Girl” and “Cover Girl”. No artist page appears. Predictive search for “banksy” ranks a Mr Brainwash work second
The nav's “Most Viewed” links to #, “Artworks” uses /collections/all, and the Artist Index has phone links but no embedded enquiry form
Templated copy is showing in public: “radiant landscapes and contemporary escapism supports collector-focused modern art collector interest”. On the artwork page: “It supports a selective, investor-facing acquisition message. It adds context.” Robert Indiana appears as “b. 1928”. Typos include “as new works. take shape” and “Book an apppointment”
Our recommendations
One hero CTA pair, and a slim sticky bar built only from the sections on the page, with a compact “Speak to an advisor” button and a scroll offset so headings aren't hidden
Search & Discovery filters for Series, Signed, Framed, Width and Height, using the price-band metafields already in place
Four exhibition states (Upcoming, Current with works, Current sold through, Past) chosen by metaobject dates. Past shows keep the sold works and switch to “Find similar works by this artist”. Group the archive by year and remove duplicates
Tune the search app's relevance with an exact-phrase boost and artist synonyms. Group predictive results into Artists, Artworks, Series and Articles, and pin artist hubs for name queries
Fix or remove “Most Viewed”, send Artworks to the agreed All Artworks page, and embed the compact enquiry form on the Artist Index
Run an editorial QA pass on every artist card and Why Collect module before scaling content. Each bio gets a named reviewer

Deep dive: AI search

Be the gallery the assistant names

When a collector asks an assistant “where can I buy a signed Banksy in London?”, the answer draws on well-structured, specific, consistent sources. Maddox has the authority but is publishing thin or contradictory signals.

What's holding visibility back
Empty artwork descriptions and an empty description in Product schema leave nothing to quote
Contradictory facts: two London main numbers, two return figures, mismatched exhibition dates
No Event schema on 83 exhibition pages, and no breadcrumbs there either
Templated artist bios that read as machine-written, which weakens trust signals for models and people alike
Our approach
Baseline in month one. 60 commercial prompts (artist + buy, artist + value, gallery + Mayfair) run monthly across ChatGPT, Gemini, Perplexity and AI Overviews, tracking mention, citation and position.
Entity pages. Artist hubs with dated, factual sections (biography, key series, market notes, FAQs) and Person plus sameAs links to Wikidata and museums.
ExhibitionEvent schema with dates, venue and performers from the exhibition metaobject, plus ArtGallery entities with separate numbers and hours.
Citable Thursday content. Articles that each answer a single question, with a summary up top and original Maddox data that nobody else can publish.

Where to start

Labelled by priority: Critical items unblock everything else, High items carry the biggest commercial impact, and Medium items build on both. Priority weighs impact on qualified enquiries and organic visibility against the effort to ship in your Shopify theme.

Priority 1 · Critical
Fix tracking and consent before anything else
GTM audit, one generate_lead event across form, chat, booking and calls, Consent Mode v2 with a real “Reject all”, and one reCAPTCHA key. Every later result depends on this.
Priority 1 · Critical
Clean up indexation
Remove the soft 404 and feed pages from the sitemap, noindex vendor and /collections pages, fix stale exhibition metadata, and set the LCP images to eager-load with high priority.
Priority 1 · Critical

Bottom line

Maddox already has what most galleries online lack: blue-chip inventory, a real advisory team and the editorial authority to be the answer when a collector searches. The Shopify migration gave you a solid base.

The opportunity is to remove the duplication the migration left behind, bring every enquiry into one measurable journey, and make each artist and artwork page specific, consistent and worth citing. That combination is what turns organic visibility into qualified conversations with collectors.
