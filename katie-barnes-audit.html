01 — Executive summary

The promise is real. It's just arriving too late on the page.

Katie Barnes does something most brands in this space don't: it pairs professional-grade tools with proper education, and insists both should be within reach at every budget. That positioning — "tools and training for every stage of your journey" — is the most persuasive thing on the site. The trouble is where it sits. It appears roughly two-thirds of the way down the homepage, beneath a rotating hero, several product rows and a kit-builder.

The first thing a visitor meets instead is a three-slide carousel: Tenner Tuesday, the desk light, the dust collector. Three strong offers, but three competing messages where there should be one. A nail tech landing cold has to work out what Katie Barnes is before they can work out what to do — and the two clearest jobs on the site (shop the tools, learn the method) aren't separated until they've already committed to a sprawling menu.

Lead with the promise. Split the two jobs early. Let the proof you've already earned do visible work.

None of this is a criticism of the work — it's the natural result of a catalogue and a community both growing quickly, with the shopfront racing to keep up. The fixes below are largely re-ordering and clarifying what already exists, not rebuilding it. Most of the highest-impact ones can ship in a week.

03 — Basket value

"Build your kit" is the right instinct. It can go further.

The shop-by-price strip is a smart way in for newer techs. The bigger opportunity sits in pairing what already lives side by side: tools with the training that teaches them, and singles with the kits that complete them.

A — Tool + training

Pair the machine with the method

Add a matching masterclass to e-file and equipment pages — "learn it properly." It lifts order value and quietly lowers returns by setting up the buyer to succeed.

B — Curated kits

Sell the kit, not just the bit

Build named starter and pro kits from the bestsellers already on the homepage, with a clear saving against buying separately. One decision instead of eight.

C — Course ladder

Lead with the complete course

Front the Complete Course bundles, with individual classes as the entry rung. Anchors value upward and routes toward the subscription rather than away from it.

04 — Navigation

07 — Roadmap

Where it could go, in order.

Week one — clarity

Fix the first screen

Settle the hero, lift the promise, add the proof bar, enrich the reviews and standardise CTAs. Low effort, and the part a cold visitor sees first — so it moves the most, soonest.

Month one — structure

Re-sequence the homepage and slim the navigation

Introduce the two-door split, re-order the page so shop and learn lead, and collapse the top-level menu to the leaner set. Front the Complete Course bundles over single £10 classes.

Quarter one — depth

Guided selling and value levers

Add a short product finder for e-files, bits and brushes. Pair tools with their matching masterclass, build named kits, and surface loyalty and course subscriptions on the homepage to work the repeat-purchase that a professional audience makes inevitable.

The opportunity

A homepage that works as hard as the brand behind it.

Taken together, these moves do one thing — they let a first-time visitor understand what Katie Barnes is, who it's for, and where to go, inside the first scroll. The promise leads, the proof you've already earned becomes visible, and the two journeys — shopping the range and learning the method — are given equal, deliberate weight.

The return is a store that converts more of the traffic you're already winning, lifts order value through considered tool-and-training pairings, and carries the authority of the brand you've built. I'd welcome the chance to talk it through, sequence it against your priorities, and shape where it goes from here.
