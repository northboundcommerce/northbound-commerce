ii

The key conversion drivers

Six levers on the product page, each drawn from the live experience and tied to how a high-value buyer actually decides. Every one is grounded in something already on the page the work is in placement, clarity and momentum, not reinvention.

01Conversion
driver
Impact · Highest leverage

The price, and the path to buying

Make the next step unmistakable.
On the page

On the Ring Infinity page the price presents as “Price upon request” rather than a clear figure with an obvious action. Enquiry is a legitimate high-jewellery convention but applied by default, it asks a ready buyer to pause and begin a conversation rather than act.

The opportunity

A two-tier model: a clear price and buy action where a piece can simply be bought; a refined, reassuring enquiry where exclusivity warrants it each with one unmistakable next step and a stated response time.

Why it converts

At this level the decision is already considered. Every step between intent and action is a moment to reconsider and the widest gap on any product page is the one between “I want this” and a clear way to have it.

The standard De Beers and Boodles reserve enquiry for genuine high jewellery and bespoke, and make that path concierge-smooth; everything else can simply be bought.

02Conversion
driver
Impact · High

Reassurance at the point of sale

Answer the quiet questions before they're asked.
On the page

The reassurance a buyer looks for is present 18K recycled gold, ethically sourced stones, a six-month warranty, worldwide shipping, a returns window but it sits within a long FAQ near the foot of the page rather than beside the price, where the decision is made.

The opportunity

Bring four or five of these into a quiet trust line directly beneath the price, and present each piece's materials and stones as proof. The content already exists; it simply belongs where it counts.

Why it converts

Buying at this level for the first time, a customer asks whether the piece is authentic, insured and returnable. Answered at the moment of decision, that reassurance carries the sale; left to be searched for, it quietly defers it.

The standard Monica Vinader and De Beers carry provenance, complimentary insured delivery and returns directly alongside the buy action reassurance offered before it must be sought.

03Conversion
driver
Impact · High

Proof, carried onto the page

Let the demand you've earned do its work.
On the page

The brand's momentum lives on social, yet none of it reaches the product page there's no rating, review or sign that others have bought and loved a piece at the moment a new customer is weighing it up.

The opportunity

Bring reviews, press and real client imagery onto the page, and place the rating high directly beneath the product name so confidence is established before the price is read.

Why it converts

Few wish to be the first to spend at this level. Evidence that others already have is among the most persuasive reassurances in luxury and the goodwill is being generated daily, simply not where it converts.

The standard Monica Vinader places the star rating immediately under the product title and treats reviews as part of the page, not an afterthought.

04Conversion
driver
Impact · Highest AOV lever

The styling story, made shoppable

You're already writing the basket.
On the page

The Ring Infinity description already styles the full look, naming the Artemis Necklace, the Manasa Earring and the Love Chain as companions. They are written about beautifully but not presented as something a customer can add.

The opportunity

A discreet “wears well with” that makes the companions your own copy recommends available in a single touch styling as service, never a hard cross-sell.

Why it converts

This is the clearest route to a higher average order value on the site. The full look is already merchandised in prose; making the pairings tappable deepens the story and lifts basket value at once.

The standard the leading houses turn “wears well with” into a quiet, shoppable invitation that reads as styling advice rather than selling.

05Conversion
driver
Impact · Medium

Delivery & availability, on the piece

Tell them when it arrives.
On the page

Whether a piece is ready to ship or made to order over four to six weeks is explained in the FAQ, yet not indicated on the individual product so a buyer cannot tell when a particular item would actually arrive.

The opportunity

A simple availability line per product “ready to ship” or a clear made-to-order timeframe placed high in the decision, framing a wait as part of the craft rather than a delay.

Why it converts

For a considered or gifted purchase, when it arrives is part of the decision. Certainty keeps the buyer moving; uncertainty sends them away to find the answer, and not all return.

The standard luxury houses state availability and crafting time on the piece itself, presenting the timeline as part of the story.

06Conversion
driver
Impact · Foundational

One clear way in

Let buyers shop the way they think.
On the page

The site offers two parallel ways to browse a “Collections” menu and a separate “EShop” categories menu of bracelets, earrings, necklaces and rings. Both are valid, but kept apart they split the same shopper intent across two routes.

The opportunity

Bring both under one considered menu by collection and by category, side by side so a visitor always knows where the piece they have in mind will be found.

Why it converts

Every moment of “which way leads to what I want” is friction before the product page is even reached. A single, legible structure brings more of your demand onto the pages that sell.

The standard the finest jewellery sites present one clear architecture, letting collection-led and category-led shoppers each find their way without thinking.
